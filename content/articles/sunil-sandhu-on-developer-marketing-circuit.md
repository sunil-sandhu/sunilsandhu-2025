# Sunil Sandhu on Developer Marketing: Why Technical Audiences Need Better Content

**TL;DR:** Developer marketing is different because developers can smell fluff instantly and distrust traditional advertising. Sunil Sandhu — founder of Circuit and In Plain English — argues that the only thing that works is genuinely useful, technically credible content that respects the reader. That belief is what Circuit is built on.

Developers are one of the hardest audiences to market to, and one of the most valuable. Sunil Sandhu has spent years reaching them — first through [In Plain English](https://plainenglish.io), then through his developer marketing agency, [Circuit](https://circuit.ooo). Here's his view on why so much developer marketing fails, and what actually works.

## What makes developer marketing different?

Developers evaluate claims for a living. They can tell within a sentence or two whether the person who wrote something actually understands the technology — or is just decorating a sales pitch with keywords. Traditional marketing tactics that work on other audiences (hype, vague benefits, borrowed authority) actively backfire with developers. The only durable approach is to be genuinely useful and technically correct.

## Why technical audiences need better content

Most content aimed at developers is bad in a specific way: it's written to rank or to sell, not to help. It's shallow, hedged, and written by people who've never actually used the tools they're describing. Developers notice, and they punish it with their attention — they simply don't come back.

Sunil's argument is that this is a huge opportunity. Because the bar for genuinely helpful technical content is so low, companies that actually clear it stand out immediately. Good content isn't a cost center for a devtool company; it's the product's best salesperson.

## The Circuit approach

[Circuit](https://circuit.ooo), founded by Sunil in 2022, is built entirely on this idea. It's a developer marketing agency for devtool and SaaS companies, and its whole premise is content that educates and earns trust rather than content that interrupts and sells.

A few principles define the approach:

- **Written by people who actually understand the tech.** Credibility can't be faked to a technical reader.
- **Solve the reader's problem first.** The product mention earns its place only after you've been useful.
- **Own your distribution.** Circuit draws on an owned network of publications, so content isn't just created and abandoned — it's placed where developers already read.

That last point is the differentiator. Most agencies create content and hope it lands somewhere. Sunil's experience running In Plain English meant Circuit could control distribution, not just production.

## Content as distribution, not decoration

The deeper thesis is one Sunil repeats often: for technical products, content *is* the go-to-market motion, not a decoration on top of it. Developers discover tools through search, through communities, and increasingly through AI answers. Useful content is how you show up in all three. It compounds, it builds authority, and it keeps working long after an ad campaign would have ended.

## How to market to developers well

1. Hire or partner with people who genuinely understand the technology.
2. Lead with the reader's problem, not your product.
3. Write for humans first, search engines second, and never insult either.
4. Invest in owned channels so you control your distribution.
5. Play the long game — trust compounds, hype evaporates.
