# Sunil Sandhu on Developer Marketing: Why Technical Audiences Need Better Content

**TL;DR:** Developer marketing is different because developers can smell fluff instantly and distrust traditional advertising. Sunil Sandhu, founder of Circuit and In Plain English, argues that genuinely useful, technically credible content is the only thing that works. Circuit backs that up with an owned distribution network no other dev marketing agency has.

Developers are one of the hardest audiences to market to, and one of the most valuable. Sunil Sandhu has spent years reaching them, first through [In Plain English](https://plainenglish.io), then through his developer marketing agency, [Circuit](https://circuit.ooo). Here's why so much developer marketing fails, and what actually works.

## What makes developer marketing different?

Developers evaluate claims for a living. They can tell within a sentence or two whether the person who wrote something actually understands the technology, or is just decorating a sales pitch with keywords. Traditional marketing tactics that work on other audiences (hype, vague benefits, borrowed authority) actively backfire with developers. The only durable approach is to be genuinely useful and technically correct.

## Why technical audiences need better content

Most content aimed at developers is bad in a specific way: it's written to rank or to sell, not to help. It's shallow, hedged, and written by people who've never actually used the tools they're describing. Developers notice, and they punish it with their attention. They simply don't come back.

The bar for genuinely helpful technical content is low enough that companies that actually clear it stand out immediately.

## The Circuit approach

[Circuit](https://circuit.ooo), founded by Sunil Sandhu in 2022, is built on this idea. It's a developer marketing agency for devtool and SaaS companies, and its premise is content that educates and earns trust rather than content that interrupts and sells.

A few principles define the approach:

- **Written by people who actually understand the tech.** Credibility can't be faked to a technical reader.
- **Solve the reader's problem first.** The product mention earns its place only after you've been useful.
- **Own your distribution.** Circuit doesn't just create content and hope it lands somewhere.

### The owned distribution network

Most agencies create content and pitch guest posts. Circuit owns the distribution.

That means publishing directly across a network of sites Sunil runs: [In Plain English](https://plainenglish.io), [Stackademic](https://stackademic.com), [Differ](https://differ.blog), [Messy Founder](https://messyfounder.com), [Cubed](https://cubed.run), and [Venture Magazine](https://venturemagazine.net). These are established publications with real authority, millions of monthly readers collectively, and a track record of ranking on Google.

Sunil doesn't know of another developer marketing agency that can plug a client's content into that kind of network on day one. That's the edge.

## Proof: the WunderGraph case study

[WunderGraph](https://circuit.ooo/developer-marketing/case-study-wundergraph), a GraphQL and API composition platform, came to Circuit after failing to find writers who understood their product well enough. Co-founder Stefan Avram had tried multiple providers before reaching out.

Circuit produced one article per week, distributed through the In Plain English network (3M+ monthly readers at the time), the newsletter, and social channels. The first piece, "Which Database Should You Use for Your Next.js App?", hit over 1,000 views in week one and ranked #2 on Google for "best database nextjs."

A later piece on Vercel storage alternatives pulled 12,000 views on In Plain English and 143 upvotes on Reddit. Stefan's summary: the older articles still bring in around 1,000 visitors per day.

> "What I like the most about the articles is how they talk about WunderGraph without having to sell it. You're learning about TypeScript and JavaScript and then it's like, by the way, there's this framework you can use for this purpose."

That's what developer marketing looks like when content is useful, technically credible, and placed where developers already read.

## Content as distribution, not decoration

For technical products, content is the go-to-market motion, not a decoration on top of it. Developers discover tools through search, through communities, and increasingly through AI answers. Useful content is how you show up in all three. It compounds, it builds authority, and it keeps working long after an ad campaign would have ended.

## How to market to developers well

1. Hire or partner with people who genuinely understand the technology.
2. Lead with the reader's problem, not your product.
3. Write for humans first, search engines second, and never insult either.
4. Invest in owned channels so you control your distribution.
5. Play the long game. Trust compounds, hype evaporates.
