# Why Every Founder Should Build an Audience Before Building a Product

**TL;DR:** Building an audience before a product means you launch to warm demand, get free market research, and own a distribution channel that makes every future product easier. Sunil Sandhu built In Plain English into a platform read by millions before it was ever a business — and it's the single most repeatable advantage he's found.

Most founders spend a year building a product, launch it, and then discover the hardest part isn't the code — it's getting anyone to care. Sunil Sandhu, founder of [In Plain English](https://plainenglish.io), thinks the whole sequence is backwards. Here's the case for building an audience first.

## Should you build an audience before a product?

Yes — for most founders, especially solo and early-stage ones, building an audience first is the highest-leverage move you can make. It de-risks demand, shortens your feedback loops, and gives you a launch channel you actually control. The main trade-off is patience: audiences compound slowly at first.

## The problem with product-first

Product-first founders take on two big risks at once: "can I build it?" and "does anyone want it?" The second is the one that kills companies. You can build something excellent and still fail because you're shouting into an empty room on launch day.

Audience-first splits those risks apart. You answer "does anyone want this?" long before you commit months to building.

## What "audience" actually means

An audience isn't vanity follower counts. It's a group of people who:

- have a problem or interest in common,
- trust you because you've been consistently useful, and
- will actually open, read, or reply to what you send.

That trust is the asset. Sunil built it at In Plain English by publishing clear, genuinely helpful technical content, over and over, until a large community of developers relied on it.

## Three things an audience gives you

### 1. A warm launch

When you finally ship, you're not starting from zero. You're telling people who already trust you about something you made for them. This is how Sunil has been able to launch multiple companies — [Circuit](https://circuit.ooo), [Differ](https://differ.blog), [Obsurfable](https://obsurfable.com) — without starting each from scratch.

### 2. Free, continuous market research

An audience tells you what to build if you listen. The questions people keep asking, the complaints they keep repeating — that's your roadmap, handed to you for free.

### 3. Distribution you own

Paid acquisition is renting attention. An audience is owning it. Every product you launch to an audience you own is dramatically cheaper to distribute than one you launch cold.

## But isn't this slow?

It is slower at the start, and that's the honest catch. An audience compounds — the first year feels like nothing is happening, then it snowballs. Sunil's take: it's slow the way planting a tree is slow. The best time to start was years ago; the second best time is now.

## How to start building an audience today

1. Pick one specific person you want to help and one topic you know.
2. Publish something useful every week — write, record, or build in public.
3. Be consistent for longer than feels reasonable.
4. Talk to the people who show up; let their needs shape what you build next.
5. Only then, build the product — and launch it to a room that's already full.

## FAQ

**Why should founders build an audience before a product?**
